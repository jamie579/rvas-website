## Using these checklists

1. Choose the correct tool and power type below.
2. Work through the checks and practice together. Mark each point **Covered and understood**, **N/A** or **Needs more help**.
3. Record the agreed task, limits and outcome. The member and demonstrator each enter their own name and date as a **typed sign-off**.
4. Enter the submitter’s email address carefully and submit. Google Forms sends a copy of the answers to that address, and RVAS stores the record in its private Google Drive.

**If more help is needed, no solo use** until the outstanding points have been addressed and recorded. These forms are induction records, not booking forms or confirmation that a particular machine is available.

The demonstrator must know how to use the machine safely and explain it clearly. Allow time for questions and practice according to the member’s experience. Observe the member checking, handling, starting, using and stopping the machine. Explain any N/A answers and record the agreed limits.

### Emergency arrangements

Complete and explain the first-aid kit location, site address or postcode and emergency entrance or access in the form during the induction. If someone is injured, stop and make the area safe without putting yourself at risk. Get first aid; call 999 or 112 for serious injury and arrange access for responders. Report accidents and near misses to the Tool Librarian.

### Committee checks before adopting these sheets

Check each sheet against the actual model’s manual, including PPE, fuel, cutting attachment, safety distances and isolation. Record any model-specific limits and resolve discrepancies before use. Make the correct manuals accessible, arrange suitable checks and maintenance with a competent person, and keep faulty tools out of use.

The source guidance records that **written insurance confirmation is still to be obtained**. Confirm cover for the machinery, tasks, member borrowing and volunteer demonstrations, including operator or age restrictions and whether cover is liability, personal accident or both. Keep the insurer’s response with the policy.

The 18+ limits shown on the sheets and the chainsaw certificate and authorisation requirements are society safeguards, not statements of a universal legal requirement. Follow any stricter insurance conditions.

### Keeping records and refreshing skills

Keep your emailed copy. RVAS keeps the submitted record with its tool induction records; individual answers are not displayed on this website. Revisit the induction after a relevant change, incident, concern or a long gap in use. Before every use, check the machine and working area and stop if unsure.

For help, a missing copy or a correction, contact the Tool Librarian at [rvascontact@gmail.com](mailto:rvascontact@gmail.com).
